Studywise

A free, immersive study space that also keeps track of how much you actually study.

Date 2026
Role Designer & Developer
Stack Next.js, TypeScript, CSS
The Problem

High school and university students rarely have one place to see how long they have studied and another place to practise study habits like the Pomodoro technique. Most students juggle a timer, a notes app and a task list, and none of them show how study time adds up over a week.

The tools that do cover both tend to feel flat or hold back what students want most. A bright screen with white noise that doesn't match what you're looking at is not immersive, and the weekly study graph is often locked behind a paywall. The most immersive option I found only runs on iOS, which leaves anyone studying on a laptop out.

Research

What Flocus and FocusFlight get right, and where they stop.

Before designing anything I reviewed two study apps. Each one solved half of the problem.

Flocus

Flocus is a web app that pairs the Pomodoro technique with white noise. Its features are easy to reach and the look is sleek and minimal, and it includes a task list, an ambient forest mode and a Pomodoro study mode. The bright background is harsh on the eyes and can pull focus away from studying, though, and the home page shows only the time even though most people come for the timer. The weekly study graph is behind a paywall.

FocusFlight

FocusFlight turns each session into a virtual flight between real airports, so a London to Paris focus session lasts as long as the real flight. Booking a flight, tearing the ticket and boarding creates a clear "mode switch" before a session, and cabin noise plays throughout. It is iOS only with no web version, and it tracks miles and badges but has no dashboard of hours per day or week, no streaks and no study goals.

How Studywise compares

Flocus has the tracking but not the atmosphere, and FocusFlight has the atmosphere but not the tracking. Studywise combines both and runs in a browser.

The Opportunity

What if the immersive study app and the study tracker were the same free product?

Studywise is built for high school and university students who want somewhere to study, a way to build habits like Pomodoro and a clear record of their effort. The main features are free so nobody has to pay to see their own study time. Detailed statistics could sit behind an optional paywall, and I could add a donation link similar to Wikipedia's.

The Solution

A short path from opening the site to studying.

When students open Studywise they choose between a themed study session and a blank Pomodoro session, then say whether they are studying for a particular course. They study, and the minutes are logged to their account automatically. When they're done they can leave, and their stats and leaderboard position are there next time.

Key Features

Quantify Your Study Time.

Studywise tracks how long you study for each subject and shows graphs of those sessions across the week. This answers the missing dashboard in FocusFlight and the paywall in Flocus.

A ranked leaderboard shows how long everyone has studied each week and resets every Sunday at 11:59 pm. Immersive themes let you study in a cafe, library, train, plane or car with audio to match. For transport themes you can show a progress bar of your trip or choose a blurred view out of the window. Finally, the Pomodoro timer is adjustable, so students can change the study and break lengths to fit their session.

Visual & Audio

Dark and quiet, so the environment stands out.

Flocus's bright background was the first thing I wanted to avoid. Studywise uses dark grays with a small palette and a single soft blue-purple accent, so nothing competes with the study session. Audio follows the theme, and on the home page you can choose any sound from the catalog.

The Build

Designed and built end to end.

I designed the product and wrote the code myself using Next.js, TypeScript and plain CSS, with the site hosted on Netlify and connected to GitHub. Building it myself meant I could test every design decision in the browser straight away.

Choosing a trip uses a searchable destination combobox, so you can type a place instead of scrolling a long list. Routes come in curated sets, with duration keys prefixed by transport mode so a train trip and a flight can't be mixed up. Ambient audio follows browser autoplay rules, so sound starts from a user action instead of being blocked. A dimness and volume panel lets students tune how immersive the screen feels, and a focus mode removes everything except the session.

What's Next

Where Studywise goes from here.

The next step is user testing. Watching students use the site will show whether the themes help them focus or distract them. After that I want to add more environments and a bigger sound catalog to keep long-term users from getting bored, and to build out the detailed statistics that could be offered as an optional upgrade while the core product stays free.
